PHASE TRANSFORMATIONS

Microstructures in motion.

Phase-field methods describe the evolution of diffuse interfaces and order parameters. My work examines how thermodynamics, transport and anisotropy influence phase separation, precipitation, coarsening and ferroic domain evolution.

Alloys & superalloysFerroelectric oxidesPhase separation

Questions we investigate

  • How do elastic stresses alter the morphology and evolution of precipitates?
  • How do thermal and compositional gradients influence microstructural evolution?
  • How do electromechanical interactions affect domain switching in ferroelectric materials?
ELASTIC STRESS EFFECTS

Elastic Stress Effects.

My work examines elastic stress effects on microstructural evolution, including anisotropic misfit, elastic anisotropy and elastic inhomogeneity. We develop phase-field models and FFT-based microelasticity solvers to study the coupling between evolving microstructures and mechanical fields.

Anisotropic misfitElastic stress effectsFFT microelasticityDislocation dynamicsCrystal plasticity

Applications include elastic stress effects on phase separation in ternary alloys, multivariant microstructures with anisotropic misfit, and stress effects on grain boundary migration. Related work connects discrete dislocation dynamics and crystal plasticity with strengthening and deformation in heterogeneous materials.

Questions we investigate

  • How do anisotropic misfit and elastic interactions influence microstructural morphology and variant evolution?
  • How do elastic anisotropy and inhomogeneity affect phase separation in alloys?
  • How can spectral methods resolve mechanical fields in heterogeneous microstructures?

These themes connect my doctoral work on multivariant microstructures with subsequent research on alloy phase separation and computational microelasticity.

PHYSICS-INFORMED NEURAL NETWORKS

PINN Inverse Modeling.

Learn the idea step by step: interactive PINN introduction →

I develop numerical inverse methods using physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) and multiobjective optimization to extract diffusion coefficients and mobility data in multicomponent alloys from experimental diffusion profiles.

This research is carried out in collaboration with Prof. Aloke Paul’s group at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bengaluru. His group leads the experimental diffusion studies and measurements, while my group at IIT Hyderabad develops the PINN and numerical inverse-modeling methods.

Together, we connect experimental evidence with governing transport equations to infer composition-dependent diffusion coefficients and mobility data. Applications include pseudo-binary diffusion couples and multicomponent NiCoFeCr and NiCoFeCrMn alloys.

PINNsInverse diffusion problemsMobility dataMultiobjective optimization

Areas of expertise

  • Physics-informed neural network methods for inverse transport problems
  • Optimization of composition-dependent diffusion coefficients
  • Mobility data extraction from experimental diffusion profiles in multicomponent alloys

SCIENTIFIC COMPUTING

Make the physics computationally useful.

My group develops GPU-accelerated phase-field solvers and parallel numerical methods. The IITH contribution to MicroSim includes CUDA-based modules such as KKS-CuFFT and KKS FD CUDA MPI.

We also investigate machine-learning approaches to accelerate physics-based models of microstructural evolution.
