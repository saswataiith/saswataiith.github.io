THERMODYNAMICS & KINETICS · COMPUTATIONAL LABORATORY

Lab 01: Thermodynamics — From G–X curves to equilibrium phase fractions

You have learned that, when phases are in equilibrium, the chemical potential of each component must be the same in every phase. Together with conservation of each component, this condition helps us calculate equilibrium phase compositions and amounts. In this lab, I show how to use molar Gibbs energy–composition (G–X) curves and a common tangent to do this. The examples include a binary eutectic and standard elemental reference (SER) states. The lab provides plots, seven exercises, worked solutions, self-checks and a coding guide. It uses a simple model and does not require a thermodynamic database.

Transport laws and conservation equations

\[\text{potential or state}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{driving force}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{flux}\ \longrightarrow\ \nabla\!\cdot(\text{flux})\ \longrightarrow\ \text{local accumulation}.\]

A potential gradient supplies a driving force, a constitutive relation connects that force with a flux, and the divergence of the flux enters the local conservation law. The details differ for momentum, heat, mass, charge and microstructural evolution, but the definitions and units must be specified for each problem.
