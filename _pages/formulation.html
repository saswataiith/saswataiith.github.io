FROM MY DOCTORAL THESIS · CHAPTER 3

From variant fields
to elastic energy.

A companion to the elastic stress effects covered in my MS5033 classroom teaching. A guided view of the formulation in Evolution of Multivariant Microstructures with Anisotropic Misfit: A Phase Field Study, by Saswata Bhattacharyya.

One tensor field, several physical contributions.

The composition field c and three structural order parameters η₁, η₂ and η₃ describe the local state. Their contributions combine into an eigenstrain: the local strain the material would adopt if it were free to transform without constraint.

\[\begin{aligned}\epsilon^0_{ij}(\mathbf r)&=\sum_{p=0}^{3}\theta_p(\mathbf r)\epsilon^{(p)}_{ij}\\ \theta_0&=c,\qquad \theta_i=\eta_i^2\quad(i=1,2,3)\end{aligned}\]

Thesis Eq. (3.5), printed p. 21. Squaring each structural order parameter makes its eigenstrain contribution unchanged when its sign reverses.

Constraint turns misfit into stress.

A coherent microstructure cannot generally realize every local eigenstrain independently. The elastic strain is the difference between compatible total strain and eigenstrain. Hooke’s law then gives stress, and mechanical equilibrium determines the displacement field.

\[\begin{aligned}\epsilon^{\mathrm{el}}_{ij}&=\epsilon_{ij}-\epsilon^0_{ij}\\\sigma^{\mathrm{el}}_{ij}&=C_{ijkl}\epsilon^{\mathrm{el}}_{kl}\\\frac{\partial\sigma^{\mathrm{el}}_{ij}}{\partial r_j}&=0\\F_e&=\frac12\int_V\sigma^{\mathrm{el}}_{ij}\epsilon^{\mathrm{el}}_{ij}\,dV\end{aligned}\]

Thesis Eqs. (3.14–3.15), (3.19) and (3.21), printed pp. 26–27. Repeated spatial indices are summed.

The assumptions matter.

The thesis studies a two-dimensional, elastically homogeneous and isotropic model system. Misfit anisotropy is varied through t; it is distinct from anisotropy of the elastic stiffness. The formulation uses linear elasticity, coherent interfaces and periodic boundary conditions. With homogeneous stiffness and no applied stress, the mean strain is the volume average of the eigenstrain.

Elasticity feeds back into evolution.

The total free energy combines chemical and elastic contributions. The elastic energy therefore enters the variational driving forces for composition and structural order. The thesis couples conserved composition evolution through the Cahn–Hilliard equation to nonconserved order-parameter evolution through the Cahn–Allen equation, and solves mechanical equilibrium in Fourier space.

\[F=F_c+F_e\]

This links local transformation strains to collective microstructural evolution: variants interact through the elastic field, rather than evolving independently.
