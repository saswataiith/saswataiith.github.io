This separate, editable 24 × 24 toy pattern shows how Hoshen–Kopelman style scanning works. A gold box visits cells from left to right. Occupied cells get labels; touching labels merge and change to a common colour. The final step optionally joins periodic edges.


Click cells to draw bridges or cut channels, then replay. Try making a connection across opposite edges. Colours identify components, not composition or curvature. This four-neighbour toy labels clusters; periodic winding rank is measured separately in the full 3D analysis above.

Measure what the demonstration shows

Hoshen–Kopelman style label-and-merge scanning assigns connected-component labels, merges periodic seam neighbours and tracks unwrapped displacements. A nonzero displacement on closing a loop gives its winding vector. Three independent vectors give winding rank three. These results agree with the independent flood-fill calculation.

Measure the interface’s topology and geometry

Loading full-resolution morphology measurements…

Euler characteristic is calculated separately for the phase solid and the interface surface. The surface has χ = V − E + F after periodic seam identification; for S closed orientable components, χ = 2S − 2Σg. Area and curvature are measured before display simplification. Closure and orientation must pass before genus is reported.

These complementary measures follow the ideas studied by Fiałkowski, Aksimentiev and Hołyst. The analysis is a new implementation; it does not claim to be their historical C program. Euler characteristic combines topological counts and does not alone prove percolation. Digital 6- and 26-neighbour Euler results are both saved so corner-contact sensitivity is visible.

Reproduce the measurements without evolving the simulation

Download the saved composition field and analysis source to one folder, then run:

python -m pip install numpy scipy numba scikit-image
python connectivity_morphology.py composition.npy connectivity-output

This writes analysis.json and phase-flood.bin.gz. The latter stores one little-endian unsigned 16-bit word per original voxel: the high bit indicates red and the remaining bits give the shortest-path distance from that phase’s seed. It is lossless for the selected phase definition and distances. Both seeds reach their entire phase.

Root labels are identifiers, not geometric locations. The JSON records the actual flood seeds, cluster sizes, winding vectors, threshold checks, volume, digital Euler values and periodic interface geometry. Integrated mean curvature uses H = (k₁+k₂)/2, with the normal pointing out of the high-c phase; a convex high-c ball gives positive integrated H. Gaussian curvature is integrated through Gauss–Bonnet on the checked closed surface. These are discrete mesh estimates, not converged continuum measurements.

Generation can take several minutes and needs a few GB of memory. The browser loads a compressed connectivity volume and holds a 32 MiB distance array. Current browsers with gzip decompression support are required. The surface loads only when requested.

Simulation provenance and limits

Dimensionless Cahn–Hilliard: ∂c/∂t = M∇²μ, μ = f′(c) − κ∇²c; f = Ac²(1−c)². A = M = κ = 1; grid 256³, spacing 0.25, box length 64, timestep 0.5, 400 steps, final time 200. Seed 20261005; mean-zero Gaussian noise of standard deviation 0.01 about c = 0.5. Stabilized semi-implicit Fourier integration, stabilization S = 2, explicit nonlinear term, no dealiasing. The saved field is unclipped float32.

Connectivity is established for this saved discrete field at thresholds 0.45, 0.50 and 0.55. This does not establish numerical convergence or representativeness. Grid refinement, timestep reduction, multiple seeds and different coarsening times remain necessary robustness studies. Energy decreased at the sampled times; no unconditional-stability claim is made.
