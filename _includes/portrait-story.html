AN ANIMATION THAT STAYED WITH ME

A face, two equations,
and a reason to be curious.

During my Master’s, I saw an animation on the homepage of Prof. W. Craig Carter of MIT. What caught my imagination was not a deep theoretical argument, but an image evolving through an equation. I was really inspired. Sometimes these small encounters become powerful motivating events.

In that spirit, here is my face evolving under two different equations. Inspired by Prof. W. Craig Carter, MIT. His historical homepage shows image evolution under Cahn–Hilliard and Allen–Cahn dynamics.

My portrait is the energy minimum.

Start with a fragmented, almost cubist version of my face. Now define an energy whose minimum is the intact portrait. Both equations move downhill in that energy, and the face emerges as the end product of forward evolution.

Left: Allen–Cahn, nonconserved dynamics. Right: Cahn–Hilliard, conserved dynamics. The energy readouts fall as the portrait returns. The movie ends with a pause on the recovered face. Time sampling is nonuniform to make the early and late evolution visible; playback speed is not a physical rate comparison.

The target portrait is part of the energy definition. It is not pasted over the result at the end, and this is not reverse playback. The distorted starting image is a permutation of image blocks, so it has the same mean intensity as the target.

How do we make the portrait the minimum?

Let r(x,y) be my grayscale portrait and u(x,y) the evolving field. Choose the deliberately simple energy

\[F[u]=\frac12\int_\Omega(u-r)^2\,dA,\qquad\mu=\frac{\delta F}{\delta u}=u-r.\]

Every squared difference is nonnegative. The unique unconstrained minimum is u = r, where F = 0. Here we design an illustrative image energy; this is not the ordinary double-well free energy of a material.

\[\partial_tu=-L(u-r)\quad\text{(Allen–Cahn)},\qquad\partial_tu=M\nabla^2(u-r)\quad\text{(Cahn–Hilliard)}.\]

On the periodic domain, their energy changes satisfy

\[\frac{dF}{dt}\bigg|_{\mathrm{AC}}=-L\int_\Omega\mu^2\,dA\le0,\qquad\frac{dF}{dt}\bigg|_{\mathrm{CH}}=-M\int_\Omega|\nabla\mu|^2\,dA\le0.\]

Cahn–Hilliard preserves the mean for any initial field. Allen–Cahn does not impose that constraint: its mean approaches the target mean. For this same-mean starting image, the Allen–Cahn mean also stays fixed as a special case. Equal mean readouts here do not make both dynamics conserved.

If the starting mean differed from the target mean, Cahn–Hilliard would approach r plus a constant offset, rather than r itself. Its conserved constraint determines which minimum is reachable. Thus an abstract-looking result is not automatically a “wrong equation”: it may reflect a different constraint or energy.

We solve these linear evolution equations with exact Fourier propagators on a 256 × 256 periodic grid of width 32, with L = 0.03 and M = 1. The zero Fourier mode of the Cahn–Hilliard error stays fixed. Both methods approach the target asymptotically; the final displayed image is the computed field at time 240, not an exact pixel replacement.

Let Cahn–Hilliard make the abstract portrait.

Here both panels use conserved Cahn–Hilliard dynamics and start from exactly the same portrait with a small, zero-mean perturbation. The left energy favors my portrait. The right energy favors phase separation, so patches of color develop from the evolving fields themselves. No painted cubist image is supplied as a target.

The equation follows the energy we give it. A phase-separating energy is the wrong choice if our aim is to recover a photograph, even when the solver is working correctly. The result is an emergent abstract image, not a reproduction of Picasso’s style.

The two energies and what is conserved

We treat the red, green and blue channels as three independent signed fields. This is an image analogy, not a coupled material or perceptual color model.

\[F_{\rm portrait}=\frac12\int\sum_j(u_j-r_j)^2\,dA,\qquad F_{\rm domains}=\int\sum_j\left[\frac{(u_j^2-1)^2}{4}+\frac{\kappa}{2}|\nabla u_j|^2\right]dA.\]
\[\partial_tu_j=M\nabla^2\frac{\delta F}{\delta u_j}.\]

On the right, the portrait is used only to initialize the field: there is no portrait fidelity term or cubist target. On the left, the portrait is explicitly encoded as the energy minimum. Both runs preserve each channel’s mean on the periodic domain. The same zero-mean noise is added to each starting portrait, so the left-hand target is compatible with conservation.

Grid: 256 × 312, spacing 1, M = 1, κ = 0.4. The right-hand nonlinear evolution uses a stabilized semi-implicit Fourier step with Δt = 0.1 and stabilization 2; the left uses the exact linear Fourier propagator. The displayed energies are normalized per channel and area and belong to different functionals, so their absolute magnitudes should not be compared. Numerical values are not clipped; only displayed RGB values are clipped to the screen’s range. Conservation is checked on the numerical fields.
