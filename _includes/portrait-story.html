AN ANIMATION THAT STAYED WITH ME

A face, two equations,
and a reason to be curious.

During my Master’s, I saw an animation on the homepage of Prof. W. Craig Carter of MIT. What caught my imagination was not a deep theoretical argument, but an image evolving through an equation. I was really inspired. Sometimes these small encounters become powerful motivating events.

In that spirit, here is my face evolving under two different equations. Inspired by Prof. W. Craig Carter, MIT. His historical homepage shows image evolution under Cahn–Hilliard and Allen–Cahn dynamics.

My portrait is the energy minimum.

My portrait is first broken up by shuffling its pixels, producing a distorted, noise-like image. Now define an energy whose minimum is the intact portrait. Both equations move downhill in that energy, and the face emerges as the end product of forward evolution.

Left: Allen–Cahn (nonconserved), \(L=0.03\). Right: Cahn–Hilliard (conserved), \(M=10^{-5}\). Values are dimensionless.

The apparent noise is my portrait broken up into shuffled pixels. As the field evolves toward its energy minimum, the recognizable face returns.

How do we make the portrait the minimum?

Let r(x,y) be my grayscale portrait and u(x,y) the evolving field. Choose the deliberately simple energy

\[F[u]=\frac12\int_\Omega(u-r)^2\,dA,\qquad\mu=\frac{\delta F}{\delta u}=u-r.\]

Every squared difference is nonnegative. The unique unconstrained minimum is u = r, where F = 0. Here we design an illustrative image energy; this is not the ordinary double-well free energy of a material.

\[\partial_tu=-L(u-r)\quad\text{(Allen–Cahn)},\qquad\partial_tu=M\nabla^2(u-r)\quad\text{(Cahn–Hilliard)}.\]

On the periodic domain, their energy changes satisfy

\[\frac{dF}{dt}\bigg|_{\mathrm{AC}}=-L\int_\Omega\mu^2\,dA\le0,\qquad\frac{dF}{dt}\bigg|_{\mathrm{CH}}=-M\int_\Omega|\nabla\mu|^2\,dA\le0.\]

Cahn–Hilliard preserves the mean for any initial field. Allen–Cahn does not impose that constraint: its mean approaches the target mean. For this same-mean starting image, the Allen–Cahn mean also stays fixed as a special case. Equal mean readouts here do not make both dynamics conserved.

If the starting mean differed from the target mean, Cahn–Hilliard would approach r plus a constant offset, rather than r itself. Its conserved constraint determines which minimum is reachable. Thus an abstract-looking result is not automatically a “wrong equation”: it may reflect a different constraint or energy.

We solve these linear evolution equations with exact Fourier propagators on a 256 × 256 periodic grid of width 32, with L = 0.03 and M = 0.00001. Frames use nonuniform simulation times, so playback is not a physical rate comparison. The zero Fourier mode of the Cahn–Hilliard error stays fixed. Both methods approach the target asymptotically; the final displayed image is the computed field at dimensionless time 30,000,000, not an exact pixel replacement.

The energy determines the destination. The same conserved dynamics can restore a portrait or form color domains, depending on the free-energy functional.

The number shown under each panel is its dimensionless mean energy density: that panel’s total energy (F), divided by the image area and by the three RGB channels. It is not an interfacial energy in J m(^{-2}). Since the panels use different energy functionals, compare how each number decreases in time, not its magnitude against the other panel.

The two energies and what is conserved

We treat the red, green and blue channels as three independent signed fields. This is an image analogy, not a coupled material or perceptual color model.

\[F_{\rm portrait}=\frac12\int\sum_j(u_j-r_j)^2\,dA,\qquad F_{\rm domains}=\int\sum_j\left[\frac{(u_j^2-1)^2}{4}+\frac{\kappa}{2}|\nabla u_j|^2\right]dA.\]
\[\partial_tu_j=M\nabla^2\frac{\delta F}{\delta u_j}.\]

On the right, the portrait supplies the initial field, which evolves under a double-well and gradient energy. On the left, the portrait is explicitly encoded as the energy minimum. Both runs preserve each channel’s mean on the periodic domain. The same zero-mean noise is added to each starting portrait, so the left-hand target is compatible with conservation.

Grid: 256 × 312, spacing 1, M = 1, κ = 0.4. The right-hand nonlinear evolution uses a stabilized semi-implicit Fourier step with Δt = 0.1 and stabilization 2; the left uses the exact linear Fourier propagator. The displayed quantity is (F/(3|\Omega|)), a dimensionless mean energy density. The two values belong to different functionals, so their absolute magnitudes should not be compared. Numerical values are not clipped; only displayed RGB values are clipped to the screen’s range. Conservation is checked on the numerical fields.
